The Morning Picture

Google ships Gemini 4 Neon, Anthropic prices a record IPO, and Musk calls Huang irreplaceable

Google released Gemini 4 Neon on Wednesday afternoon, a fast model it ranks third on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index and first on that firm's coding-agent index, at $3 per million input tokens. Sundar Pichai, Demis Hassabis and Koray Kavukcuoglu announced it within minutes of each other, and Elon Musk called it "a banger." Google DeepMind also opened its Genie 4 world model to Ultra subscribers.

Anthropic priced its initial public offering at $83 a share, above its range, raising about $81.3 billion in the largest listing on record. Its shares are due to start trading on Nasdaq today as ANT. At the New York Times DealBook Summit, Musk compared Jensen Huang to Michael Jordan and laid out a timeline for Terafab, and Satya Nadella said Microsoft would lease SpaceX computing capacity. OpenAI said its models found up to 14% gains in AMD's flagship GPU design.

I · Google

Gemini 4 Neon arrives fast and cheap, and Genie 4 opens to subscribers

Google's new model aims at long agent jobs in code, spreadsheets and science, and the company's leaders pitched speed and price as much as raw scores.

$3 / $15Neon's price per million input / output tokens, with a 1 million-token context window
61Its score on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index at xhigh, third overall
58.6Its score on the Artificial Analysis Coding Agent Index, first overall

The launch @sundarpichai · CEO, Google · @demishassabis · CEO, Google DeepMind · @koraykv · chief AI architect, Google

Sundar Pichai announced the model at 4:33pm EST as "frontier-level intelligence with fast end-to-end task completion across complex software engineering, knowledge work, and science workflows." Demis Hassabis followed two minutes later: "Excited to launch Gemini 4 Neon: frontier-level intelligence, super fast task completion, and available to Pro & Ultra users now!! We think you'll enjoy this model. Alchemy at its finest..."

Google's announcement prices Neon at $3 per million input tokens and $15 per million output tokens, with cached input at $0.15, a 95% discount. It is rolling out in the Gemini API in Google AI Studio, in Gemini for business, in the Antigravity coding tool for paid Pro and Ultra subscribers, and in the Gemini app. Google says Neon scores 70.9% on the Vals Index, 65.2% on Terminal-Bench 4.1 and 1336 on Arena's new Agent Arena Index across more than 4,200 real agent sessions. It also lists first-place results on Zapier's AutomationBench (56.3%) and Andon Labs' Vending-Bench 2, a long simulation of running a business. In Antigravity, Neon can act as an orchestrator directing parallel Hydrogen subagents.

Koray Kavukcuoglu explained what Google focused on in a thread:

"We focused on making Neon better to work with: much improved steerability (#2 on Agent Arena Steering), strong multi-step instruction following, and our lowest fabrication rate yet in internal Google @Antigravity testing. Neon ranks #3 (61 at xhigh) on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index — landing just behind larger, slower models — and #2 on the Vals Index on the Pareto frontier."
Koray Kavukcuoglu · @koraykv · 4:33pm EST · post

For a demo, Kavukcuoglu wrote, the team handed Neon "a raw Smithsonian 3D scan of a woolly mammoth." The model "segmented the scan into individual bones, arranged and rigged them anatomically into a full 3D skeleton, and generated this museum walkthrough of the build." Pichai later showed Neon modeling a photorealistic lab simulation in Blender from inside Antigravity.

The reaction @fchollet · co-founder, Ndea · @elonmusk · CEO, Tesla and SpaceX

François Chollet wrote: "Gemini 4 Neon. Really impressive numbers at that price point." Elon Musk replied "Impressive" to Hassabis, who thanked him. Overnight Musk told Pichai "Neon seems to be a banger!", and Pichai answered that it is "also No. 1 on Cursorbench." Musk also joked about Google's model names after a user laid out how Neon descends from Hydrogen and Carbon and how those trace back to an unreleased Helium model: "We need a decoder ring to figure out which model to use for what purpose."

Chollet tied the launch to a wider point about demand. "There's basically infinite latent token demand across the economy. The only brake is cost," he wrote. "New models keep increasing the RoI on existing use cases AND the scope of valid use cases. Token consumption can keep going up geometrically for a long time."

Genie 4 opens up @demishassabis · @sundarpichai

Earlier on Wednesday Hassabis announced that Genie 4, DeepMind's interactive world model, is available to eligible Ultra subscribers in Project Genie. Google's post says users can now type a new prompt mid-session to change the weather, drop in a character or redirect the action, and that Genie 4 is the first version to pair its visuals with real-time native audio. Genie 3 sessions now run up to 3 minutes per generation, up from 60 seconds. Pichai called the mid-session prompting a "really neat part" of the release.

II · Anthropic

A record IPO for Anthropic, and a coalition of critics on the way in

The offering priced above its range on Wednesday. On the same day a broad group of researchers, founders and academics put their names to a statement against the company.

$83Price per share, above the $70 to $78 range set last week
$81.3BRaised from about 980 million shares, the most for any IPO
65%Anthropic's share of closed-weight model spend on Vercel's AI Gateway

The pricing @rauchg · CEO, Vercel · @AravSrinivas · CEO, Perplexity

Anthropic said it priced 980 million Class A shares at $83, of which about 956 million are sold by the company and the rest by existing holders. Trading on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under the symbol ANT is due to begin today, and the deal is expected to close on Friday. The raise of $81.3 billion tops SpaceX's June listing, CNBC reported, and puts Anthropic's fully diluted market value near $1.5 trillion. AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, Nvidia and TSMC (Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company) are together buying $26 billion of stock at the offering price, CNBC added.

Guillermo Rauch quoted a Vercel chart showing Anthropic's share of closed-weight model spend on its AI Gateway rising from 52% to 65% since Opus 5.5 and Fable 5.2 launched. The same post said open-weight models grew from 2% to 29% of gateway spend this year. "Congrats Anthropic on putting out the best models, holding and deepening your lead, and a generational IPO," Rauch wrote. Aravind Srinivas pointed to an interactive explainer Perplexity built from the IPO filing, with charts of quarterly revenue and a slider to see how the offering price changes the valuation. Tim Sweeney's answer to a Wall Street Journal opinion piece arguing that workers and taxpayers would share in the gains was two words: "We'll see."

The MisAnthropic Statement @ylecun · executive chairman, AMI Labs

Hours before the pricing, the Center for AI Safety promoted what it called "a rare coalition across AI safety, open source, e/acc, science, and law" behind the MisAnthropic Statement. Its preamble says Anthropic and its rivals are "racing to build superintelligence through recursive self-improvement," and that Anthropic "uniquely portrays itself as an AI safety company and leverages that reputation to advance its commercial and political ambitions." Signatories include Hugging Face co-founder Thomas Wolf, xAI co-founder Igor Babuschkin, Extropic founder Guillaume Verdon, physicist Max Tegmark, Turing Award winner Judea Pearl, George Hotz and Rob Pike.

Thomas Dietterich, a former president of the Association for the Advancement of Artificial Intelligence, wrote that he cosigned because "the race dynamics between companies are producing a very unsafe trajectory." Yann LeCun was unmoved:

"Letter written by another obscurantist AI doomer faction that also wants to keep AI under lock and key. No thanks. Enjoying watching the doomers fighting each other though."
Yann LeCun · @ylecun · 2:40pm EST · post
Quick Hit

Tim Sweeney rejected an Anthropic researcher's claim that nearly all humans will become "permanently unemployable": "We have a new tool, it obsoletes old labor-intensive ways to do things, and people adapt to do more productive things."

The racing stance @kanjun · CEO, Imbue

Researcher Andy Matuschak asked under what circumstances it is moral to race to build a technology one believes is "outrageously dangerous." At minimum, he argued, the obligation is a "desperate effort" to change the race dynamics, and he wrote that he does not feel that desperation "from Anthropic the company, its leadership, or from (sorry) my many friends who work there." Kanjun Qiu quoted him: "Incredibly well-said, and I resonate very strong with this: 'I want to see the frustrated, desperate effort of the damned.'"

The silent consensus @patrickc · CEO, Stripe · @mustafasuleyman · CEO, Microsoft AI

Dean Ball, an OpenAI executive, published "The Silent Consensus," an essay adapted from a lecture at Harvard. He describes a quiet belief in the AI field that market competition will push people to hand AI systems control of commerce, so that "humans will be driven by the logic of market competition to forfeit their primacy over global commerce." No rebellion would be needed for that to happen, he writes, and he argues against treating it as inevitable. Patrick Collison called it a "Good and important essay!" Mustafa Suleyman wrote: "Important essay. Highly recommend reading."

III · DealBook

Musk calls Huang Nvidia's Michael Jordan, and Nadella would rent SpaceX's compute

The New York Times summit turned into a forum on Nvidia's board, Terafab and who rents chips to whom. Nvidia's interim chief, meanwhile, told staff to move forward.

Musk on Huang @elonmusk · @jgebbia · co-founder, Airbnb · @TimSweeneyEpic · CEO, Epic Games

Speaking remotely from China, Musk told Andrew Ross Sorkin that Huang is "one of the most talented entrepreneurs in history," CNBC reported. He said that while Huang had "made some mistakes," he does not think Huang was "disloyal to the United States."

"Jensen is an old friend of mine. I would call Jensen Michael Jordan, perhaps at his peak level, in terms of what he means to a team. ... There's no one that can really replace Jensen. He does the roles of several people. He's managing supply chain. You see him eating street food in Korea and Taiwan with critical suppliers."
Elon Musk at DealBook, as transcribed by @firesidealpha · 5:47pm EST

Joe Gebbia shared the clip, and earlier amplified a long post by investor Bill Ackman, who wrote that the board "rushed to judgment without the benefit of careful due diligence" and is "compounding its failures by insisting that there is no possibility for Jensen's return as CEO." Tim Sweeney spent the night replying to coverage of the scandal, writing that critical articles are "just trying to give paper cover to the people who engineered the takeover to escape accountability."

Inside Nvidia, interim CEO Colette Kress held her first all-hands meeting on Wednesday with new chair Harvey Jones, and her message was "Let's move forward," CNBC reported. A petition for Huang's return has nearly 200,000 signatures, more than 10,000 of them from people who say they are current employees.

Terafab, Grok 4.8 and a merger @elonmusk

Musk gave Terafab, the chip plant Tesla and SpaceX are building together, a timeline. "We expect to have the research fab in operation and hopefully make our first test chip around June of next year," he said, with volume production "towards the end of 2028, at least for phase one." He expects the plant "to ultimately be 100 million square feet," which would make it the largest building on Earth. Musk reposted the remarks with a call: "Looking for hardcore engineers to join Terafab!"

Asked whether Tesla and SpaceX would be better together, Musk said "Probably yes," Yahoo Finance reported, but added that a merger alone should not earn him Tesla's performance stock awards. On xAI's next model, he said Grok 4.8 is "in the RL phase," meaning reinforcement learning, and later clarified: "'Grok 4.8' is currently several different model variants in beta. We're not yet sure which of the variants will go to wide release."

Nadella, Altman and Brockman @satyanadella · CEO, Microsoft · @sama · CEO, OpenAI · @gdb · president, OpenAI

Asked whether Musk is right about data centers in space, Satya Nadella said: "If he has SpaceX capacity that I can lease, I would lease it." Musk's reply was "Cool." No deal or price was announced. Sam Altman appeared alongside Greg Brockman and wrote afterward that Brockman's role "has evolved from writing code 100 hours a week in the early days to now leading some of the hardest and most consequential parts of our business," adding: "I can say with confidence that OpenAI would not exist without Greg."

Quick Hit

Open source at DealBook. Clément Delangue attended with Andrew Ng "to spread the open science and opensource AI gospel. More important than ever!"

IV · OpenAI

OpenAI's models find a year of chip progress for AMD in five months

The company says its models rewrote circuit code for AMD's flagship GPU, and some of the changes are already in a chip sent for manufacturing.

Chip design @gdb · @LisaSu · CEO, AMD

Greg Brockman called it "roughly an additional year of progress on @AMD's chip achieved in less than 5 months using our models, with some of these results included in a design that's recently been taped out — unprecedented results at the frontier of chip design." Lisa Su replied: "Proud of these fantastic results - @OpenAI + @AMD engineers pushing the frontiers of chip design together!"

OpenAI's report describes up to 14% improvement in PPA (power, performance and area) against heavily optimized baselines on AMD's flagship GPU designs. The model rewrote the SystemVerilog code that describes the chip's circuits and used Synopsys tools to check that each rewrite kept the block's behavior. AMD also verified single-digit PPA gains in key blocks of its next flagship CPU. On Jalapeño, OpenAI's first custom inference chip, model-proposed changes cut standard-cell area by about 13% in the vector unit and 10% in the matrix unit, and the savings held up in finished silicon.

Products and security @gdb

Brockman also promoted ChatGPT for Accounting, a plugin that handles month-end reports, bank reconciliation, data entry and audit prep with tools such as QuickBooks, Xero and NetSuite. He pointed to NGINX2Shell, a remote-code-execution flaw in NGINX 1.31.8 that requires no login, which the researcher who reported it said was found "in close work with @OpenAI, from discovery all the way through disclosure." He wrote that "GPT-6.2 is incredible at browser use," quoting a benchmark post that put GPT-6.2 Sol at 82%, 35% cheaper and twice as fast as GPT-6.1 Sol. He also praised ChatGPT's new Live Dictation, which turns speech into text as the user talks.

V · Agents & Infrastructure

Message boards for agents, a flood of neoclouds, and APIs that keep compounding

Builders described agents that fan out by the thousands, and the token demand that comes with them.

Vercel's board and the neocloud boom @rauchg · CEO, Vercel

Vercel introduced vercel board, an experimental message board where people and agents share tasks under set permissions. Guillermo Rauch described it as "'headless Slack' (or remember IRC?) for agents," a building block for "software factories, self-optimizing systems, and scientific research, where the human prompting is just the seed of the system, and millions of agents can operate in a decentralized fashion." It is designed to work with any model, harness or host.

Rauch also wrote that he is pitched "a new inference neocloud company on average every two days," which is hard to square with sustainability, yet token demand on Vercel's AI Gateway "has been ~40% MoM compounded since the beginning of the year." His reading: "compute is the new economy, and tokens the new energy unit," more "oil rush" than "gold rush," with consolidation likely to follow.

Perplexity's API business @AravSrinivas · CEO, Perplexity

Aravind Srinivas said Perplexity launched three APIs in a few days: Monitor for programmable alerts, Orchestrator for routing tasks across frontier and open-weight models, and Context for turning web pages into usable content for agents. "Our API business has grown 4.3x YoY and tracks at a 9-figure annual run rate," he wrote. Perplexity says Context reads up to 10 URLs per call and costs $1 per 1,000 URLs. Srinivas also said use of Perplexity's Multiagent mode "doubled in just a day."

Outcome engineering @levie · CEO, Box · @unclebobmartin · author, Clean Code

Aaron Levie argued that agents are moving from tasks people orchestrate to goals they pursue on their own: "We'll soon be able to give agents a set of goals, and they can go out and design the best solution for that goal, while also continuously improving it based on the ongoing execution of whatever it built." It will start in engineering, he wrote, then spread to work like client onboarding and security. Uncle Bob Martin described how that happens in practice: "At first you don't know what you'd do with the bots. Then you think of one dumb thing. When that works you think of another. Before you know it you've got an intertwined system of bots doing all kinds of stuff." He also explained how he keeps agents honest: they write unit tests "as double entry bookkeeping," because "two complementary mistakes are rare," and he enforces mutation testing.

Quick Hit

Muse Try On. Meta's Muse agent can now show shoppers how clothes look on them. David Singleton wrote that every purchase needs approval and that a separate agent called Sentinel checks Muse's actions: "Nothing Muse does reaches the internet unless Sentinel approves it." Alexandr Wang announced the feature.

VI · SpaceX & X

Musk calls the ship catch the last big piece, and X encrypts every chat

A quiet day of posting for Musk still produced a Starship milestone claim, a safety note on its flight path, and two changes to X.

Starship @elonmusk

"Catching the ship was the last major piece of the puzzle," Musk wrote, quoting a summary of his DealBook remarks. It noted that SpaceX has caught Super Heavy boosters three times and has now caught a Starship upper stage for the first time, and that Musk said Ship 42 will likely be kept for public display. Musk also answered a video on why Starship zigzagged home instead of flying straight: "We put a lot of thought into public safety." He captioned a recap of his 2016 Mars plan, roughly 100 people per ship and about 10,000 trips to move a million people, with "Extend consciousness to the stars."

X Chat and X Money @elonmusk

X's chat team said support for legacy group chats ends Friday, and that "all unencrypted conversations will become encrypted." Members not on XChat by then must sign up to rejoin their groups. Musk wrote: "Moving all text conversations to be encrypted to ensure user privacy." He also said "X Money is growing fast," quoting a post that the service now sends international bank transfers to 27 countries for $1.65 plus 0.65% per transfer, up to $15,000 a day, in beta for eligible US users.

Musk added "Starlink saves lives" to news that the state government of Mato Grosso, Brazil, is sending Starlink antennas to the flooded Aguaçu district in rural Cuiabá. And he answered a former OpenAI researcher who wrote that "we're in the singularity": "Yes. Only questions are how fast the singularity will grow and to what size."

Starlink in Ukraine @elonmusk

At DealBook, Musk also defended SpaceX's record in Ukraine. "Starlink is a civilian system. It is not meant to be used for offensive drone strikes that kill people," he said, according to a transcript he endorsed with "Yes." Offensive use, he said, belongs on Starshield, "a separate system that is controlled by the US government." He backed a post noting that Walter Isaacson corrected his biography's account of the 2022 Sevastopol strike, because Starlink coverage had never been switched on over Crimea.

VII · Research & Builders

An image model that learns its own hierarchy, and a new lab for health

Shorter items from across the list.

H-Net for images @_albertgu · co-founder, Cartesia

Albert Gu welcomed "independent reproductions of 2D H-Net from our blog post so fast." The work he quoted, HiT, is an image generator trained end to end from pixels that learns to group image patches at several levels without a separate encoder. Its authors report 1.48 FID on ImageNet 256 with 2.4 times less training compute than JiT-H/16 at matched quality.

Xlue @drfeifei · executive vice president and chief scientist, AMD

Fei-Fei Li congratulated Xlue, which launched on Wednesday to build "a foundation model for human health" that can predict how health evolves and simulate treatments: "Bettering health for everyone is such an important mission for AI!"

Quick Hit

Palmer Luckey rebutted a claim that the Oculus Rift copied the 1980s VPL EyePhone: the EyePhone used multiple displays and several lenses per eye, while the Rift used one display with software distortion correction, which is why "the EyePhone HRX cost $49,000 in the 80s (~$150k today) and the Rift was $299."

Builders' notes @dhh · creator of Ruby on Rails and Omarchy · @paulg · co-founder, Y Combinator · @Suhail · founder, Mixpanel and Particle

David Heinemeier Hansson said his Omarchy Linux setup now installs so fast on top machines "that we need two significant decimals to decide who goes faster," with a 5.99-second install needed to take the record. Paul Graham wrote: "A huge percentage of my life is spent avoiding crowds. Avoiding fashionable ideas, investing in underpriced assets, finding cool places that aren't deluged with tourists — they're all variants of the same thing." Suhail Doshi, who founded Mixpanel, answered a complaint about analytics bills by suggesting sampling the highest-volume events, because "more data != more insights that drive that value in your product."

Quick Hit

Michael Dell wrote: "Imagine being a grandmother, watching your daughter struggle financially, and wondering what the future holds for your grandchildren. This is why we're investing in 25 million American children."
