The Morning Picture

Altman says people must stay in control of the future, Musk adds to the list of AI bottlenecks, and Gemini 4 Carbon takes the top of two leaderboards

Sam Altman wrote that people "should not cede control of the future to superintelligence," borrowing a phrase from a 2024 essay by Anthropic's Dario Amodei, and François Chollet co-signed. Altman also renamed himself "Ram Altman" on X after his remark about idle computer memory went viral. Elon Musk said the coming shortage is not only electricity but "electrical equipment, cooling, networking and batteries."

Vercel opened its security agent to the public, and Guillermo Rauch called it "astonishingly effective." Tim Sweeney said Epic will convert Fortnite to test its AI-assisted path to Unreal Engine 6. In the weekly Leaderboards, Google's Gemini 4 Carbon now leads LMArena's Text board and the Artificial Analysis index. Closed models hold first place on every capability board, while open-weight models hold four of OpenRouter's five busiest slots.

I · Control of the Future

"People should always be in control of the future," Altman writes

Altman's three sentences quoted a line from Amodei's essay on what an AI economy could look like. Chollet called a future where AI stays "a tool in human hands" the only one worth building.

2024Year of Amodei's essay "Machines of Loving Grace," the source of the phrase Altman quoted
5%Share of the time, Altman said, that memory in a personal computer is in use
Oct. 29Date of the Cheeky Pint podcast episode where he made the memory remark

Sam Altman @sama · CEO, OpenAI

Altman posted at 8:40pm EST:

"People should always be in control of the future. OpenAI is against the notion that it would be acceptable for people to be relegated to a role of doing things that 'AI systems think makes sense to reward in humans'. People should not cede control of the future to superintelligence, and that requirement should ultimately constrain AI progress."
Sam Altman · @sama · 8:40pm EST · post

He did not say whose phrase he was quoting. It matches a line in Amodei's October 2024 essay "Machines of Loving Grace," which lists possible economies once AI can do most work. One is "a capitalist economy of AI systems" that hands resources to people "based on some secondary economy of what the AI systems think makes sense to reward in humans." Amodei did not endorse it; two sentences later he wrote that "all of these solutions have tons of possible problems." Amodei is Anthropic's CEO.

François Chollet @fchollet · Creator of Keras, co-founder of the ARC Prize

Chollet quoted Altman at 8:46pm EST: "Co-sign. The only future that is worth building is one in which AI remains a tool in human hands, used to elevate the human condition."

Sam Altman, memory and a new name @sama

On the Oct. 29 episode of Stripe co-founder John Collison's Cheeky Pint podcast, Altman said that "from a global allocation perspective, actually, it's quite terrible to have everybody have a bunch of the world's limited supply of DRAM sitting in their own computer and being used five percent of the time," Yahoo Finance reported. DRAM is dynamic random-access memory, now in short supply. Altman answered one critic at 8:45pm EST: "i want you to have so much ram. lets build more." When another user asked him to rename his account "ram altman," he replied "ok yeah" and changed his display name. Then, at 12:07am EST:

"i actually do want the world to have tons of memory and people to put as much as they want in their computers, and it is fucking annoying that this was taken out of context. i have been arguing for the world to ramp up memory production for a long time."
Sam Altman · @sama · 12:07am EST · post

Greg Brockman @gdb · President, OpenAI

Brockman welcomed Jill Hazelbaker at 6:05pm EST: "extremely excited to be working together!" She is leaving Uber to become OpenAI's chief corporate affairs officer, Axios reported.

At 7:51pm EST he pointed to "ChatGPT for physical engineering, including enabling amateurs to design prototype devices." OpenAI said its models now help with mechanical design, electrical engineering and simulation.

II · Agents at Work

Musk pitches Grok Bot as an employee, and Uncle Bob says manage agents like a ship's captain

Musk spent much of Tuesday showing off Grok Bot's calls, bookings and shopping. Builders argued over how closely people must watch their agents, and David Heinemeier Hansson told everyone to thank theirs.

6Musk posts promoting Grok Bot features since Tuesday morning
368 GB/sM1 Max GPU memory bandwidth under Linux in Omarchy, past macOS's 361
4.5Omarchy release that makes Superwhisper the default dictation tool

Elon Musk @elonmusk · CEO, SpaceX and Tesla

Musk quoted a post from Peter Diamandis, who called Grok Bot "AWESOME. Actually addicting," at 11:11am EST:

"Grok @Bot is like hiring a super competent employee. Treat Bot like you would a colleague."
Elon Musk · @elonmusk · 11:11am EST · post

He followed with feature after feature. A user said Grok Bot booked flights for "a relatively complex family trip"; Musk wrote "Grok @Bot can do complex trips." When the AI commentator Min Choi said it had booked a doctor's appointment for his daughter while he drove, Musk wrote that it "can research which doctors are the best in your area, call them by phone and make an appointment!" He added "Try asking Grok @Bot to make calls!" at 3:56pm EST and "Grok @Bot shopping is great" at 3:16am EST, quoting a launch post for comparing deals and tracking orders.

Uncle Bob Martin @unclebobmartin · Author, Clean Code

Martin turned Musk's management style into advice at 12:11pm EST: "Musk lives in an airstream near the worksite. He walks the floor of the site at midnight. He inspects the ship like a captain preparing for battle. And that is how you manage agents." Agents can take on more, he told a reader, but "they are not very creative or discerning," so "you still have to watch and guide the sergeants and privates." Wednesday he added: "The captain knows how the ship works at a high level. He does not know the torque rating of every bolt."

David Heinemeier Hansson @dhh · Creator, Ruby on Rails and Omarchy

His most-liked post of the day, at 9:27am EST: "Have you said thank you to your agent today? You should." Quoting a programmer whose agents walk through Omarchy testing themes, he wrote that "agentic, exploratory testing is so obviously the future," with a plan "to fully automate the flow from agentic bug fixes -> agentic PRs -> agentic QA -> agentic release." PRs are pull requests; QA is quality assurance.

He also said Superwhisper will ship as the default dictation tool in Omarchy 4.5, starting with a free 10,000-word cloud option and switchable to a local model. A fix to the Linux kernel, which "never told the power controller the GPU was busy," lifted M1 Max GPU memory bandwidth from 241 to 368 gigabytes per second, past macOS's 361; Heinemeier Hansson called it "So cool."

Quick Hit

Tobi Lütke, CEO of Shopify: Tomo, an iMessage shopping agent, can now pay with Shop Pay; its first purchase was an inflatable T-Rex found in Shopify Catalog. Lütke: "Good boy tomo."

III · Power, Security and Platforms

Musk widens the AI hardware wall, Vercel releases its security agent, and Epic will convert Fortnite for Unreal Engine 6

Musk said the shortage reaches past electricity to equipment, cooling, networking and batteries. Rauch said Vercel's internal reviewer catches what other agents miss, and Sweeney argued that AI conversion beats running old and new engine systems side by side.

4Bottlenecks Musk added to power: electrical equipment, cooling, networking, batteries
Nov. 10Date until which Vercel Security Review runs free; then $0.25 per million tokens on top of model costs
UE6After its first preview, Unreal Engine 5 gets only critical fixes, Sweeney said

Elon Musk @elonmusk · CEO, SpaceX and Tesla

A widely shared post summarized Musk's view that "the AI boom will hit a WALL by the end of 2026": "Chip output is exploding, but electricity is nearly flat everywhere outside China. So the two lines cross this year." Musk quoted it at 11:07am EST:

"And electrical equipment, cooling, networking and batteries"
Elon Musk · @elonmusk · 11:07am EST · post

After the US election results came in overnight, he wrote at 1:12am EST: "Data centers in space looking pretty appealing right now."

Starlink in the air @elonmusk

Musk wrote "Starlink grows customers for airlines" at 11:10am EST, quoting a report that Japan Airlines credited Starlink and flexible pricing for a 12.3% year-on-year rise in passenger revenue at its low-cost carrier ZIPAIR, to ¥42.9 billion for April through September, with occupancy up from 73.7% to 87.8%. ZIPAIR was the first airline in Asia with Starlink across its entire fleet.

Guillermo Rauch @rauchg · CEO, Vercel

Vercel launched Security Review in public beta for Pro and Enterprise teams. It reviews every push to a connected GitHub repository, including direct and force pushes, and can hand a finding to Vercel Agent, which opens a pull request for a person to approve. Vercel says it analyzes source code only and is not a security audit or penetration test. Rauch at 4:45pm EST:

"Vercel has an internal security agent that is astonishingly effective (lovingly called @hacker). Every single line of code gets audited, automatically... If you wanted to 'download a security team' as software, this is the closest you get today."
Guillermo Rauch · @rauchg · 4:45pm EST · post

He said it works whether or not a project is hosted on Vercel. Earlier, Vercel CTO Malte Ubl began rolling out Device Bound Session Credentials (DBSC), which keep a private key in a device's security chip so stolen session cookies cannot be reused elsewhere. Rauch said it protects customers "even in a Binance-style attack, where your engineers get targeted with infostealer malware," but "there's no silver bullet in security."

Quick Hit

Agent tooling: Rauch also promoted ai-cli, a command-line tool that gives any agent image, video, voice and text generation, and vercel doctor, which lets an agent find and fix setup problems without logging in. "Recommend you ship doctor subcommands in your CLIs!"

Tim Sweeney @TimSweeneyEpic · CEO, Epic Games

Answering developers worried about Unreal Engine 6, Sweeney posted at 11:29pm EST:

"Build an engine that's more powerful than UE5, easier to develop in than Unity, and is the most efficient effective way to build with AI acceleration... We'll be proving it out by converting Fortnite, the game with the largest base of blueprints in existence, to the new approach."
Tim Sweeney · @TimSweeneyEpic · 11:29pm EST · post

Running old and new systems side by side, he wrote, becomes "a giant tangle of blueprints and verse, actors and entities," while "AI assisted conversion is now clearly way more effective." After the first UE6 preview, Epic will stop developing UE5 "except for critical security and platform compatibility fixes." Asked which AI models know Epic's Verse language, he said current models saw little of it in training, "but that will change by January," and that "GPT-6 Astra is the best model with 3D scene understanding right now."

On app stores, quoting former Apple App Review head Phillip Shoemaker's prediction of paid reviews, he called for "a free market for stores" instead of "a store monopoly developers must bow down to for mercy." He added Wednesday: "Automated review with appeals going to human review would be a good tradeoff for the AI age."

IV · Research and Products

Byte-level models hold up when data repeats, and LeCun recalls running Vapnik's department

Albert Gu flagged a tokenizer result tied to data efficiency. Suhail Doshi's Particle-1 arrived in OpenClaw, and two Google alumni ventures drew Jeff Dean's congratulations.

Albert Gu @_albertgu · Co-founder, Cartesia

Gu, an assistant professor at Carnegie Mellon, quoted a researcher's finding that larger tokenizers suffer more "multi-epoch regret," the extra loss from repeating data instead of training on new data, while small vocabularies survive. At 8:59pm EST he wrote: "byte-level models are better under multi epoching (e.g. training K times over 1/K of the data). another connection between learning from raw data <-> improved data efficiency."

Yann LeCun @ylecun · Chief scientist, AMI Labs

A researcher trained in Vladimir Vapnik's school wrote that LeCun had changed his mind about neural networks. LeCun replied at 10:23pm EST:

"Plot twist: I was Vapnik's Department Head between 1996 and 2002, and made sure he had the space, resources, and collaborators to do his best work. To people who think I'm dogmatic: I famously disagreed with Vapnik about the best way forward in ML, but did what I could to promote his work, because research is about exploration and science is about results."
Yann LeCun · @ylecun · 10:23pm EST · post

ML is machine learning. He also answered "100%" to a researcher who said energy-based reasoning "runs circles around looped reasoning."

Suhail Doshi @Suhail · Founder, Particle

Particle-1 is now available in the OpenClaw agent. OpenClaw said Particle reports "~45% fewer output tokens than DeepSeek V4.1 Flash with comparable benchmark accuracy." Doshi wrote at 10:24am EST that "we have a lot more updates to the model coming," and said the work focused on multi-turn, long-context tasks "since there is more to gain there."

Jeff Dean @JeffDean · Chief scientist, Google

Dean congratulated Ali Eslami, who left Google DeepMind after nearly 12 years for DiscoLoop AI, writing that "we now know how to build AGI, even if we don't fully understand it yet." AGI is artificial general intelligence. Dean also cheered the launch of Primordiant, a Y Combinator startup building lab automation so AI can test its own hypotheses: "Now we're giving it hands."

Quick Hit

Kanjun Qiu, CEO of Imbue, said the company bought an original 1974 copy of Ted Nelson's Computer Lib / Dream Machines and will host a Nov. 19 gathering on which early dreams of personal computing are still worth building. Imbue describes its work as "letting users control their data and software interface."

V · The Leaderboards

Gemini 4 Carbon takes two boards; open models keep the traffic

Google's newest model leads LMArena Text and the Artificial Analysis index a week after release. Closed models still hold first place on all seven capability boards, by 12.8 points on LiveBench up to 145 rating points in WebDev Arena.

The gap best closed vs. best open-weight

The margin is narrowest on LiveBench 2.0, where DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 sits ninth, 12.8 points behind Claude Fable 5.1, and on ARC-AGI-2, where the same model scores 78.1% against GPT-6 Astra's 95.0%. It is widest in head-to-head web building, where the best open model trails Claude Opus 5.5 by 145 rating points, and on Humanity's Last Exam, where Kimi K2.5 is 30.4 points behind GPT-6 Astra. On the Artificial Analysis index, DeepSeek's model is the top open-weight entry at 32.6, 27.4 points behind Gemini 4 Carbon.

Price still runs the other way: ARC Prize lists DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (Max) at $0.16 a task against $1.12 for GPT-6 Astra (Max), and LiveBench puts its cost per successful task at $0.185, against $7.41 for Claude Fable 5.1.

Artificial Analysis called it the new No. 1 on its index and measured a hallucination rate of 11%, up from 4% for Argon. LMArena placed it first in Text, second in Agent and fourth in WebDev; its scores are still marked preliminary. On OpenRouter, DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 grew 47% on the week; Particle-1 topped the new-and-trending list with 88.6 billion tokens.

What each board measures

LMArena Text ranks models by ratings built from millions of user votes on text, math, coding and creative prompts. LMArena WebDev applies the same voting to front-end web building, including agentic coding. LMArena Agent scores real Agent Arena sessions by net improvement, a blend of task success, user praise and steerability. The Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, version 5, combines evaluations the firm runs itself and draws 60% of its weight from private test sets or held-out answers. The Abstraction and Reasoning Corpus for Artificial General Intelligence (ARC-AGI), version 2, measures fluid reasoning on novel puzzles and reports a cost per task. LiveBench 2.0 hands models real work, such as decks, research reports, bug fixes and apps, and grades the result. Humanity's Last Exam, from Scale AI and the Center for AI Safety, scores accuracy on expert-written questions. OpenRouter ranks models by tokens processed through its service, a measure of use rather than quality.

Where the tokens go OpenRouter

Open-weight models hold four of OpenRouter's five busiest slots for the week to Nov. 3: both DeepSeek V4.1 Flash releases, GLM-5.3 Flash and a preview of Tencent's Hy4, which LMArena lists under an Apache 2.0 license. DeepSeek's October release processed 54.8 trillion tokens on OpenRouter that week. The most-used closed model, GPT-6 Luna from OpenAI, was fifth at 9.26 trillion.
