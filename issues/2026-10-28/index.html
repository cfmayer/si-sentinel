The Morning Picture

Google's Genie 4 builds worlds that talk back, and Anduril bets on an American laptop

Google DeepMind opened Genie 4, a world model that renders playable scenes with physics, sound and characters who speak, and uses Gemini to reason about what should happen next. The same day Anduril and Qualcomm unveiled BlueChip, a rugged laptop that Palmer Luckey called "the first American computer in decades," though The Verge found most of its parts still come from abroad.

Elsewhere, a startup finished a machine-checked proof of the classification of finite simple groups, a result that once filled about 15,000 pages. Tim Sweeney explained why Epic will let AI companies train on Unreal Engine output, Elon Musk said Starbase Louisiana alone could fly 10,000 Starships a year, and the weekly leaderboards changed shape as Artificial Analysis rebuilt its index.

I · World Models

Genie 4 adds sound, speech and reasoning to generated worlds

Google DeepMind's newest world model lets a user steer a live scene, hear it and talk to the people in it. It is open to trusted testers now, with a waitlist for everyone else.

LiveScenes respond to text prompts and controls in real time
AudioNative sound and characters who converse, a first for Genie
GeminiReasons through multi-step events before the world renders them

Sundar Pichai @sundarpichai · CEO, Google and Alphabet

Pichai introduced Genie 4 at 12:15pm EDT Tuesday as "our most realistic and interactive world model." His list of new abilities: steering events, camera angles and actions as a scene runs; generating audio matched to the world, "from realistic background sounds to characters who can converse in real time"; photorealistic scenes with real-world physics; and "more coherent multi-step events through reasoning from Gemini."

"Prompt 'traffic light green,' and Gemini reasons through what happens next as the signal changes: the engine revs, and the motorcycle accelerates down the coast."
Sundar Pichai · @sundarpichai · 12:15pm EDT · post

Access is narrow for now. Pichai said the model is rolling out as a research preview to trusted testers, and is "coming soon to Google AI Ultra subscribers in Project Genie and early access partners via the Gemini API and AI Studio."

Demis Hassabis @demishassabis · CEO, Google DeepMind

Hassabis, whose lab built the model, joined in at 7:59pm EDT and pointed to the larger question that world simulators raise:

"Simulations within simulations, makes you wonder just how deep the rabbit hole goes... I have a feeling we will be finding out soon enough..."
Demis Hassabis · @demishassabis · 7:59pm EDT · post

Three minutes later he added that wider access is "coming soon" and directed people to the waitlist linked from the blog post.

II · Hardware

Anduril and Qualcomm unveil BlueChip, a laptop built in America

The defense company's first computer runs a Snapdragon X Series chip and is aimed at troops and field crews. How American it is depends on who is counting.

~32%Share of the prototype's component cost of US origin, per The Verge
90%+US and allied share, with circuit boards assembled at Arsenal-1 in Ohio
2027Planned launch, early in the year

Palmer Luckey @PalmerLuckey · Founder, Anduril

Anduril introduced BlueChip with Qualcomm as a rugged laptop for people working on "critical problems." Luckey made the patriotic case at 1:46pm EDT Tuesday:

"BlueChip is the first American computer in decades. Don't let anyone lie to you and say it can't be done. Designed in California. Built in America."
Palmer Luckey · @PalmerLuckey · 1:46pm EDT · post

The Verge reported that about 32% of the prototype's component cost is of US origin and more than 90% comes from the US and its allies, with circuit-board assembly at Anduril's Arsenal-1 plant in Ohio and a launch planned for early 2027. Luckey called the piece "full of blatant misinformation" at 8:48pm EDT, disputing its account of which parts he said would be American-made from the start.

He also said the supply chain is meant for others to use. Anduril, he wrote at 1:10pm EDT, is "making sure to avoid any exclusivity agreements on the American components" it lines up. Replying to a defense writer at 5:39pm EDT, he argued that "our cost of capital alone is so much lower" than at older defense contractors. He also confirmed "it runs Crysis" and opened a list for the first batch.

Cristiano Amon @cristianoamon · CEO, Qualcomm

Amon said at 4:09pm EDT that "Snapdragon X Series powers the first laptop, designed and assembled in the U.S." Qualcomm's release framed the project as a start on rebuilding American computer manufacturing.

Tim Sweeney and David Heinemeier Hansson @TimSweeneyEpic · @dhh

Sweeney, chief executive of Epic Games, saw a gap in the market at 12:05am EDT Wednesday: "As much of mobile computing has evolved into fragile art, there's a huge unmet demand for durable work tools." Heinemeier Hansson, the creator of Ruby on Rails, asked at 6:39pm EDT to get his Omarchy Linux setup "running on this thing," and Luckey offered to ship him one.

Hemant Taneja @htaneja · CEO, General Catalyst

The factory theme ran through the day. Taneja said at 11:05am EDT that Re:Build Manufacturing raised $2.1 billion, co-led by General Catalyst and JPMorgan's Security and Resiliency Initiative, arguing that if the US wants to "turn its intelligence lead into hardware, it needs manufacturing infrastructure of its own."

III · Agents at Work

Bots join the payroll, and the code reviews get thinner

Builders described agents moving from demos into daily work, along with the rough edges they still show.

Michael Truell and Elon Musk @mntruell · @elonmusk

Truell, chief executive of Anysphere, the company behind Cursor, wrote at 3:26pm EDT: "Early days, but we're beginning to hire Bots onto our team." Musk promoted new features in Grok Bot at 12:31pm EDT. When a user reported a problem, Truell replied "Looking into this," and Musk answered at 2:19pm EDT: "Major fail, will get fixed pronto."

Aaron Levie @levie · CEO, Box

Levie put a number on how little of business has been automated, at 12:51am EDT Wednesday:

"The vast majority of enterprise workflows have not actually had agents applied to them in any meaningful way. The economy probably has this deployed < 1% right now."
Aaron Levie · @levie · 12:51am EDT · post

He blamed the models more than slow adoption, and said computer use, long-running agents and better harnesses now let companies "start to apply agents at more complex workflows."

Quick Hit

Uncle Bob Martin (@unclebobmartin), author of "Clean Code," on checking what his agents write, at 10:02am EDT Tuesday: "I don't review the code. I have tools that measure the quality of the tests and structure."

Uncle Bob Martin @unclebobmartin · Author, "Clean Code"

Martin, a leading advocate of test-driven development, said at 3:35pm EDT that agents write tests, "sometimes they even write the tests first," but never follow its tight write-one-test, pass-one-test loop, even when told to. His guess is that the agent's idea of a small step is bigger than his.

"The end results have been good so far, so I see no reason to interfere."
Robert C. Martin · @unclebobmartin · 3:35pm EDT · post

Noam Brown @polynoamial · Researcher, OpenAI

Brown said a Massachusetts Institute of Technology class in which students spend a month building poker AIs has been compressed: "Coding agents have accelerated that process so much that it's now possible to build a poker bot in an *hour*." He plans to attend the finals.

IV · Mathematics

A machine checks the enormous theorem

Axiom Math says it has formalized the classification of finite simple groups in the Lean proof language, a proof that took a century of human work.

43.1MLines of Lean in the finished formalization
24 daysTime the system took to produce it
34Places where the formal proof had to deviate from the published one

Kevin Weil @kevinweil · Co-founder, Warp Sciences

The classification of finite simple groups lists every basic building block of symmetry in mathematics. Axiom Math's report says its system turned the proof into 43.1 million lines of Lean in 24 days and logged 34 deviations from the literature, with a public blueprint of the whole structure. Weil, at 10:47am EDT Tuesday:

"The original proof was 15,000 pages of painstaking work across 500 papers by 100 mathematicians. Now settled beyond a doubt."
Kevin Weil · @kevinweil · 10:47am EDT · post

Tim Sweeney @TimSweeneyEpic · CEO, Epic Games

Sweeney marked the result at 4:57pm EDT with a nod to the mathematician John Conway, who worked on the classification and invented the Game of Life: "I wish group theorist (and creator of the finite automata 'game of life'), John Conway were still here to see this."

V · Training Rights

Epic will let AI train on Unreal Engine output

Tim Sweeney explained a coming change to the engine's license, and where it stops.

Tim Sweeney @TimSweeneyEpic · CEO, Epic Games

Epic plans to drop the clause in its end-user license agreement that bars training AI on what Unreal Engine and MetaHuman produce, Game Developer reported; the current agreement still prohibits it in section 6(e). Sweeney laid out the legal logic at 8:10pm EDT: under the reasoning of the court ruling in the Anthropic copyright case, training on engine output likely would not infringe copyright, but it would breach a contract the AI company had agreed to.

"Our upcoming EULA change removes that contractual restriction on training on our, Epic's, engine outputs."
Tim Sweeney · @TimSweeneyEpic · 8:10pm EDT · post

He drew a line at 12:26am EDT Wednesday: the change "has nothing to do with Fortnite island creator code or content," and anyone wanting to train on a creator's work must sign a deal and pay. The payoff for developers, he said, is that AI assistants "will become way more familiar with working in the engine." Each game studio can still set its own license terms.

VI · Launches and Tools

New models from Microsoft, a payments rail from Stripe

A busy day of releases across images, security, search, deployment and money.

Mustafa Suleyman @mustafasuleyman · CEO, Microsoft AI

Suleyman said at 11:12am EDT that MAI-Image is now "the #2 image edit model in the world," available on the MAI Playground; LMArena's Image Edit Arena put MAI-Image-2.7 third with a rating of 1,470. At 1:10pm EDT he announced MAI Cyber 1.1 Flash, saying the cyber model has "doubled user adoption to 50% in the last 30 days" inside Microsoft's security harness and is 20% more token-efficient.

Aidan Gomez @aidangomez · CEO, Cohere

Gomez highlighted results from the customer-service company ada, which runs Cohere Embed on Model Vault: 77-millisecond median retrieval latency for voice agents, 2.7 times the throughput per dollar, a vector store four times smaller and 90.7% top-10 recall, per Cohere's case study.

Quick Hit

Patrick Collison (@patrickc), chief executive of Stripe, at 4:45pm EDT Tuesday: "A2A payments will be more important than P2P payments." Stripe Pay, which lets businesses pay one another instantly by handle, launched the same day.

Guillermo Rauch @rauchg · CEO, Vercel

Rauch announced at 7:06pm EDT, in the form of a terminal session, that Vercel can now deploy a container from a plain Dockerfile. His four-line demo served a page that read "we are so back."

David Heinemeier Hansson @dhh · Creator, Ruby on Rails

Heinemeier Hansson shipped the second release candidate of Omarchy 4.5, his Linux desktop setup, at 7:42pm EDT, saying it is "slimmer, lighter, and WAY WAY FASTER TO INSTALL!"

David Singleton @dps · Co-founder, Muse

Singleton said at 12:38am EDT Wednesday that Muse is now available in Singapore.

VII · Space

Starlink reaches phones across Brazil, and Musk sizes up Louisiana

A direct-to-phone deal with Brazil's largest carrier went live, and Musk put a number on a second Starbase.

Elon Musk @elonmusk · CEO, SpaceX and Tesla

Musk posted "Starlink Brasil" at 10:46am EDT Tuesday as Vivo launched Vivo Satélite, which connects ordinary phones directly to Starlink satellites where there is no cell signal. CNN Brasil reported that it reaches 50 million of Vivo's 105 million mobile customers at launch, free for 12 months on eligible plans, and supports text messages, audio, maps and messaging apps but not video. Teletime said business customers get three free months. Starlink told CNN Brasil that 7 million people connected during the week of testing.

At 2:07am EDT Wednesday he made a claim about launch rates:

"10k Starship launches/year is just from Starbase Louisiana"
Elon Musk · @elonmusk · 2:07am EDT · post

For scale, the Federal Aviation Administration's request for information on new spaceports projects 10,000 licensed launches and reentries a year across the whole country by 2035, up from 217 US launches in 2025. Comments closed on Oct. 26.

Quick Hit

John Carmack (@ID_AA_Carmack), on a reply predicting that a robot running an open-source model could handle a jack-o'-lantern chore end to end by Halloween 2028, at 11:19am EDT Tuesday: "I'll give you 2:1 odds against that happening."

VIII · The Leaderboards

A rebuilt index widens the open-weight gap; open models still carry the traffic

A closed model leads all seven capability boards, by 11.7 points on LiveBench up to 143 rating points in WebDev Arena. Artificial Analysis rebased its index overnight, and open-weight models hold four of OpenRouter's five busiest slots.

37.3TTokens DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 processed on OpenRouter in the week to Oct. 27

The gap best closed vs. best open-weight

The margin is narrowest on LiveBench 2.0, where DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 sits ninth, 11.7 points behind Claude Fable 5.1, and on ARC-AGI-2, where the same model scores 78.1% against GPT-6 Astra's 95.0%. It is widest in head-to-head web building, where the best open model trails first place by 143 rating points, and on Humanity's Last Exam, where Kimi K2.5 is 30.4 points behind. On the rebuilt Artificial Analysis index, DeepSeek's model and Zhipu's GLM-5.3 tie at 32 among released open weights, 26 behind Claude Opus 5.5.

Price still runs the other way: ARC Prize lists GPT-6 Astra (Max) at $1.12 a task and DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (Max) at $0.16.

Index v5 Artificial Analysis

Artificial Analysis launched Intelligence Index v5 at 10:11pm EDT Tuesday. It adds FrontierCode 2.0, Cognition's private coding test graded on whether a maintainer would merge the change, and Terminal-Bench-Science, and upgrades to a 1,000-question Humanity's Last Exam Diamond set. The firm said the open-weight gap widens from 3 points to 12 when Kimi K3.1 Pro is counted; its weights are not yet published.

On ARC-AGI-2, DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 now tops the open-weight entries, ahead of last week's leader, Dots3-Note Preview, at 76.8%. LiveBench published a new release Oct. 25 with lower scores across the top; Claude Fable 5.1 still leads.

What each board measures

LMArena Text ranks models by ratings built from millions of user votes on text, math, coding and creative prompts. LMArena WebDev applies the same voting to front-end web building, including agentic coding. LMArena Agent scores real Agent Arena sessions by net improvement, a blend of task success, user praise and steerability. The Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index combines evaluations the firm runs itself; version 5, launched Tuesday night, draws 60% of its weight from private test sets or held-out answers. The Abstraction and Reasoning Corpus for Artificial General Intelligence (ARC-AGI), version 2, measures fluid reasoning on novel puzzles and reports a cost per task. LiveBench 2.0 hands models real work, such as decks, research reports, bug fixes and apps, and grades the result. Humanity's Last Exam, from Scale AI and the Center for AI Safety, scores accuracy on expert-written questions. OpenRouter ranks models by tokens processed through its service, a measure of use rather than quality.

What a run costs Artificial Analysis

Among the 10 highest scorers on the new index, the cost of running the full test suite per task ranges from $0.96 for GPT-6.1 Terra at maximum effort to $13.04 for Claude Fable 5.1, by the firm's figures. Twenty of the 76 models it lists are open-weight.

Meta's open model Muse Spark Open

Meta's newly released open-weight Muse Spark Open, with 816 billion parameters and 42 billion active, went through Artificial Analysis testing on Oct. 23. It scores 25 on the new index and sits 54th in WebDev Arena at 1,526, well behind Meta's closed Muse Wonder, third on the Text board.

Where the tokens go OpenRouter

Open-weight models hold four of OpenRouter's five busiest slots for the week to Oct. 27: both DeepSeek V4.1 Flash releases, GLM-5.3 Flash and a preview of Tencent's Hy4. DeepSeek's October release grew 224% on the week.

ARC-AGI-3 ARC Prize 2026

In the ARC Prize competition on the newer, interactive ARC-AGI-3 test, Yi-Chia Chen retook first place with 71.48% on Oct. 26, up from 67%, with one week left.

Terra's price OpenAI

GPT-6.1 Terra, released Oct. 26 at $1 per million input tokens and $5 per million output, is the first Terra-class model since GPT-5.6; Artificial Analysis said it is "more intelligent for less than a third of the cost." It scores 51 on the new index, fifth overall.
