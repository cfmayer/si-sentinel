The Morning Picture

SpaceX catches a Starship from orbit; the builders sign on to bring Jensen Huang back

On Saturday afternoon Ship 42 fell out of the sky over Starbase, Texas, and the launch tower's arms closed around it, the first time an orbital upper stage has been caught on its return. It came 22.5 hours after liftoff, 14 orbits and 26 Starlink satellites later. Demis Hassabis called it "true alien tech," Sundar Pichai said it "still feels like science fiction," and Elon Musk, who inspected the heat shield from a boom lift the next day, said the ship "could probably be reflown with a few weeks of refurbishment."

The weekend's other campaign ran the other way. An open letter asking Nvidia's board to reinstate Jensen Huang passed 175,000 names, and Musk, Tim Sweeney, Tobi Lütke, David Heinemeier Hansson, Joe Gebbia and Guillermo Rauch all signed or cheered it on. Nvidia's Bryan Catanzaro said a letter using his name was fake and backed the board. Nvidia reports third-quarter results on Tuesday, its first in 33 years without Huang.

Tomorrow · Tuesday, Nov. 17

Nvidia reports third-quarter results, its first without Jensen Huang

Nvidia will post results for the quarter that ended Oct. 25 at about 4:20 p.m. EST and hold its call at 5 p.m. EST, with written commentary from Colette Kress, now interim CEO as well as chief financial officer, per the company. Its guidance last quarter called for revenue of $108.0 billion, plus or minus 2%, with no data center compute revenue assumed from China, according to its second-quarter filing. The letter's organizers are urging institutional investors to raise Huang's exit on the call, The Next Web reports.

I · Nvidia

"Stand with Jensen": a letter, a fake signature and a fight over sourcing

Four days after Huang resigned, builders on the list spent the weekend signing a petition for his return, arguing that the reporting behind his fall was thin, and warning about what Nvidia loses without him.

175,000+Names on the open letter by Monday, per The Next Web
8,766Signers who say they work at Nvidia, all self-reported
$3.10Hourly H100 rental price, up from $1.96 a year ago, per Michael Dell

The letter @elonmusk · @jgebbia · @tobi · @dhh · @rauchg

The Stand with Jensen letter, from people who describe themselves as current and former Nvidia employees, partners and shareholders, asks the board to "bring Jensen back while an independent investigation establishes the facts," to disclose the inquiry's scope and timeline, and to give a "timely public explanation." It says it is not endorsed by Nvidia or by Huang. Replying to a post that urged Nvidia engineers to resign in protest, Musk wrote at 3:51pm EST: "A petition is underway to reinstate Jensen and I’m signing it too." It drew about 228,000 likes.

Airbnb co-founder Joe Gebbia posted one word, "Reinstate." Lütke answered Musk with "You have my sword," Rauch wrote "Co-sign," and on Sunday, when the organizers said Huang was reportedly considering a comeback, Lütke posted "Signed" and Heinemeier Hansson wrote "I couldn't sign this fast enough!" His earlier post drew about 19,000 likes:

"We need @JensenHuang back at NVIDIA. The free world depends on it."
David Heinemeier Hansson · @dhh · 10:22am EST · post

Inside Nvidia @ctnzr · vice president, applied deep learning research, Nvidia

Not everyone at the company joined. Bryan Catanzaro wrote at 2:28pm EST:

"There is a made-up letter circulating online asking NVIDIA’s board to reinstate Jensen, claiming my involvement. This letter is obviously fake, and even if it were real I would not sign it. I am grief-stricken that Jensen is no longer with NVIDIA. At the same time, I fully support NVIDIA’s board and have confidence in their decisions."
Bryan Catanzaro · @ctnzr · 2:28pm EST · post

He added that he has worked with Kress for years and that "She has my full support." The count has been messy: Business Insider reported organizers removing fake signatures, The Next Web notes, and Sweeney said the form "doesn't validate email or identity. I just signed it and saw that someone else had already put my name in earlier." Later that afternoon Catanzaro replied to a cancer patient who said Huang had got him into a clinical trial: "Jensen does care deeply about people."

The sourcing fight @TimSweeneyEpic · CEO, Epic Games

Sweeney spent the weekend on the reporting. After New York Times reporter Tripp Mickle said his first tip came from a "tech industry contact," Sweeney asked: "So a lobbyist at an AI company that wants to do regulatory capture?" He told The Wall Street Journal's opinion page it had created "the impression that senior government officials anonymously provided information about spying" (about 22,000 likes), and after Singapore's government said no investigation of the businesswoman at the center of the story had taken place, he wrote that the Journal had "fabricated that claim too." On Sunday he laid out his argument at length:

"The proper interface between government and private citizens and their corporations is rule of law. Laws are passed, and if wrongdoing as alleged, then there's due process: an investigation followed by facts and evidence presented in open court. ... Press takedowns full of sketchily sourced claims and backtracking narratives are not the way."
Tim Sweeney · @TimSweeneyEpic · 11:46am EST · post

In the same post he put the "running cost" of the episode at "$1.8 trillion in losses to American pension funds and shareholders," and urged opponents of Huang and of open models to see that this "isn't a win for 'team regulation'." Palmer Luckey wrote that the Times "fabricates quotes and then refuses to correct them for years on end."

Washington weighs in @TimSweeneyEpic

On Sunday Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent told Fox News that Nvidia needs a "dramatic shakeup," urged the board to pick an outsider as CEO and demanded answers within 10 days on whether its chips were diverted to China, Forbes reported. Hours later the Journal reported that the administration plans to name Nvidia, Amazon, Microsoft, OpenAI, Anthropic and others among more than two dozen AI "national champions" to help them sell abroad, according to Reuters. The program promises faster export licenses, government support overseas and federal financing. Sweeney replied at 5:54am EST: "The government shouldn’t pick winners and losers in markets nor CEOs of companies," warning that the label "risks tainting those companies with the appearance of state control or political alignment."

What a founder carries @elonmusk · @MichaelDell

Musk quoted a clip of SemiAnalysis founder Dylan Patel saying Huang had personally talked small suppliers into selling Nvidia their capacity, and that "with Jensen gone, these companies are looking around the market and it's like, well, Google's begging for more capacity." Musk wrote that many of those deals "were a handshake with Jensen, not a written contract," then added at 10:20am EST: "If Jensen gave me his word about compute delivery or I asked for components to be accelerated, he always came through. Sure, we’d put together a purchase order at some point later, but I never for a second thought it wouldn’t happen."

Michael Dell made the demand case with prices. "We started shipping the Nvidia H100 in 2022," he wrote. "A year ago it rented for $1.96/hour. Today: $3.10. +58%, on a chip that was supposed to be obsolete by now. So much for GPU depreciation."

Quick Hit

Suhail Doshi noted on Friday that the San Jose Denny's had taken down the plaque marking the booth where Nvidia was founded. By that night the restaurant had reversed course and said the plaque was going back up.

II · Space

Ship 42 comes home to the chopsticks

Flight 15 lost its booster to the Gulf of Mexico but brought the upper stage back from orbit to the tower that launched it. Musk spent the next day on the heat shield, and the watch list argued over who deserves the credit.

22.5 hrsFrom liftoff to catch, per SpaceX
14Orbits of Earth before the deorbit burn
26Starlink V3 satellites deployed

The flight @elonmusk · CEO, SpaceX

Starship lifted off from Pad 2 at Starbase at 5:47 p.m. EST Friday, Spaceflight Now reported. The Super Heavy booster's boostback burn "ended early due to low fuel mass," SpaceX's mission page says; its guidance triggered an automated abort and diverted it to an offshore splashdown, where it was destroyed. Musk said the "booster was slightly low on fuel," blamed "slight underperformance of engines on this flight" for the green flame seen on descent, and said the diversion was the right call: "We have the next booster almost ready for flight, but losing the tower or launch mount would potentially cost us 2 to 3 months."

The ship deployed its satellites, coasted for 14 orbits, relit a sea-level Raptor for the deorbit burn and flew back across Mexico. Three engines lit for the landing flip, and the tower's arms closed at 4:20 p.m. EST Saturday. "Good deorbit burn," Musk posted 53 minutes before landing. Afterward: "I hugged ship 42 upon its return."

What comes next @elonmusk

"Yeah, I was going over the whole Starship heat shield earlier today. Overall, it looks great. The engines look great too. Ship 42 could probably be reflown with a few weeks of refurbishment. However, many small fixes are needed to achieve immediate reusability (like an airplane), which requires zero expected refurbishment, apart from scheduled maintenance."
Elon Musk · @elonmusk · 5:20pm EST · post

He sketched the next version too. "Starship V4 will be ~70m long, which is roughly a 40% length increase and allows >60% more propellant load," he wrote, with nine engines, three of them added Raptor Vacuums. He predicted that "propellant cost per flight will drop below $500k and payload will exceed 200 tons," and said future flights "will carry over 50 Tb/s of Starlink bandwidth to orbit," against up to 26 terabits on this one.

The watch list reacts @demishassabis · @sundarpichai · @unclebobmartin

"One of the coolest things I've ever seen!! Loved the thunderclap as the starship dropped out of the heavens, before the catch. True alien tech. Huge congrats @elonmusk and the @SpaceX team! the whole stack is now proven out. Ad Astra!"
Demis Hassabis · @demishassabis · 8:12pm EST · post

Pichai's congratulations drew more than 51,000 likes. Kevin Weil wrote "Absolute goosebumps," Lütke called it a "World first achievement," and Hassabis later congratulated SpaceX engineer Lars Blackmore, who called the catch "7+ years in the making." Uncle Bob Martin, who had driven to Starbase for the launch, posted "On the roof, waiting for the catch" and then "from this moment on everything will be different."

Capital or founders @dhh · @PalmerLuckey · @paulg

Antitrust writer Matt Stoller argued that the catch is "the normal consequence of applying capital and engineering to any industry" and that "Musk isn't a genius, he's just a guy who can get capital from Wall Street." Heinemeier Hansson answered: "There are a million 'capital allocators', only one Musk." Musk asked how SpaceX can "deliver 85% of all Earth payload to orbit with Falcon, while Blue Origin, which started before SpaceX ... is not yet able to launch reliably," and why Boeing and Lockheed got "several times what SpaceX did" while "ULA delivers less than 5% of SpaceX payload to orbit." When Stoller said military engineers' work on GPS "dwarfs SpaceX," Luckey replied that he doubted "a single person who worked on the original GPS launch" agrees. Paul Graham wrote: "As an investor, I can tell you the limiting factor isn't investment. It's founder ability."

Quick Hit

United Launch Alliance confirmed on Sunday that its Vulcan rocket suffered an anomaly 10 minutes into flight and failed to put an Amazon mission in orbit. Musk replied: "Sorry to hear this. Hope you are back to flight soon."

III · Open AI & the Long Run

Open-source builders dig in, and Patrick Collison asks what people do after work

With Nvidia's open-model champion gone, Hugging Face shipped a 1.0 of the most-used local inference library and Yann LeCun pointed to a global open-model consortium. Collison posed the weekend's big question.

llama.cpp 1.0 @ClementDelangue · CEO, Hugging Face

"We’re not going to win by lobbying, plotting, or manipulating. We’re going to win by building, in the open, with the whole community! Today, with @ggerganov we’re super excited to announce the release of llama.cpp v1, the first major fully stable version of the most used library to run AI models locally, meaning on your phone, your laptop, and your workstation."
Clément Delangue · @ClementDelangue · 10:13am EST · post

The ggml team's announcement says llama.cpp and its tensor library ggml now follow semantic versioning, with a major version bump for any breaking change to the public C interface, and support more than 150 model architecture variants. The release notes list removed deprecated functions, 8-bit floating point (FP8) key-value caches, an expert cache for mixture-of-experts models and a hardened server. Delangue also called claims that Hugging Face had begun taking down open models "fud": "We haven’t changed our policies and practices in more than a year."

Power and sovereignty @ClementDelangue · @ylecun

Quoting an estimate that Anthropic's planned IPO could make about 4,250 of its 5,300 employees millionaires, Delangue wrote: "concentration of power, capabilities and wealth is the biggest risk in AI ... without much much stronger open-source, we might end up in a world with more asymmetry and inequalities than ever!" LeCun replied: "I've said this for years too."

When AI researcher Nathan Lambert warned that open models' allies "are retreating in DC" after Huang's exit, LeCun answered that "DC has no grip outside the USA. Every other country wants AI sovereignty, hence favors open models," and pointed to Project Tapestry. The AI Alliance describes it as a federated effort to co-train a shared open foundation model, with members contributing compute and local data while keeping control of it. LeCun compared it to roads and the internet: governments, industry and academia build the base model, "But then, anyone can find-tune it ... We need diverse AI assistants for the same reason we need a diverse press."

LeCun and Hinton @ylecun

Geoffrey Hinton had put LeCun and Huang among "a few crazies" who doubt superintelligence is near. "Geoff and I totally disagree on this. We're still friends," LeCun wrote, and he set out the gap on Sunday:

"The catastrophe scenarios (extinction, employment apocalypse, etc) are totally ridiculous for anyone who understands how thing work in the real world. ... Confidence in humanity is one thing Geoff and I disagree on. The other thing we disagree on is the primacy of intelligence. In the real world, intelligence is not synonymous with power and dominance."
Yann LeCun · @ylecun · 10:54am EST · post

Superintelligent AI "will have no desire to take over," he wrote, pointing to his "Objective-Driven AI Architecture" for guardrails enforced at inference time.

Quick Hit

Greg Brockman shared the first post on OpenAI's new science blog, in which physicist Nathaniel Craig describes working with GPT-6 Astra to develop a candidate lattice definition of the Standard Model.

After work @patrickc · CEO, Stripe · @paulg

Collison noted that Adam Smith and Montesquieu thought commerce made people better, and that Mozart and Dickens "were themselves deeply commercially-motivated in the production of their work." He asked for the optimists' account of society, not just the individual:

"If the premise is that nobody will need to work or produce, how confident are we that we actually get the next Mozart and Dickens? The relevant question is not the partial equilibrium of whether one individual can pursue a life of enlightened leisure, but the general equilibrium of what form a society as a whole will take on."
Patrick Collison · @patrickc · 8:04pm EST · post

"Math is a good source of examples of people who weren't motivated by money," Graham replied. Collison offered a living test case: the Gulf states, where 1 million to 2 million people don't work because of oil revenue, he wrote, citing figures from language models.

Search, not interpolation @fchollet · co-founder, Ndea

François Chollet argued that "a differentiable curve fitted via SGD can only interpolate," but that using such a curve "as a sampling guide in a search process" is no longer interpolation. Told the transformer substrate is old, he wrote that large reasoning models "are a *new idea* from 2024, implemented on top of an older computing substrate, which is how things usually go."

IV · Agents & Tools

Bots join the meeting, and Vercel builds a renderer no human would attempt

Agent launches and agent-built software from the weekend, plus a fast-installing Linux and a measured way to direct coding agents.

Grok Bot in meetings @mntruell · CEO, Cursor · @elonmusk

The Grok Bot team said the agent "can now join meetings," where users can "ask it questions, assign it work, or have it keep track of what your team decides." Michael Truell wrote at 2:16pm EST: "Within a couple of quarters, it will be normal for work meetings to be with Bots. This early beta gives a very rough glimpse." Musk shared the news, and also shared harness changes that give users "~7% more usage out of your plan, and up to ~15% more on routine runs," and said that Grok text watermarking, now off by default in the SpaceXSI API, "is only on where required by law or requested by the user."

xeet @rauchg · CEO, Vercel

Guillermo Rauch introduced xeet, "a hermetic, pixel-perfect X post renderer in safe Rust / wasm" that renders a post to an image in about a millisecond, "100x-1000x faster than booting up a browser and taking a screenshot." He called it "the kind of thing that would be wildly impractical, if not impossible, for a human engineer to pull off," and explained how it was built:

"Except xeet is structured as an 'autorepo.' An oracle boots up Chrome, and verifies tens of thousands of text layout rules. A CI canary then watches the evolution of the original, and files signed PRs for agents to act on. It was conceived from the ground up around 'verification engineering', and the result speaks for itself."
Guillermo Rauch · @rauchg · 11:27pm EST · post

Vercel also shipped Workflow SDK 5, which it says cuts latency and overhead by up to 25 times and encrypts every payload, and made its sandboxes start up to four times faster. Rauch described "how agents will ship all code": feature-gated code, "verification gates on push (proof checking, security audits)," then agents watching the metrics. Lütke's take on agent infrastructure was shorter: "pi-durable is the posix of agent programming."

Agents with acceptance tests @unclebobmartin · author, Clean Code

Martin said he is building "a derivative of FitNesse for use by agents as an acceptance testing platform," so "the customer and I and the agents will agree on what gets built. This is not AI for AIs sake." Asked whether code quality is subjective, he answered: "Code quality describes the effort required to maintain a system. High quality -> low effort. This is true whether it’s humans or agents manipulating the code."

ChatGPT Wallet @gdb · president, OpenAI

OpenAI said its dot devices can now "securely complete purchases on your behalf—with your approval." With ChatGPT Wallet, "agents pay without access to your actual card details," and every purchase requires a security check. It is rolling out to dots for Pro subscribers in the US. Greg Brockman wrote at 12:32pm EST that it works "in partnership with American Express, Mastercard, and Visa."

Omarchy in 18 seconds @dhh · creator of Ruby on Rails and Omarchy

Heinemeier Hansson said his Omarchy Linux distribution "now installs in just 18 seconds on the @nvidia N1x! And a few seconds later, you're being offered to download an awesome local model to run." By Monday morning a user had set a six-second record on an AMD Ryzen 7 9800X3D desktop.

Particle @Suhail · founder, Mixpanel and Particle

Suhail Doshi said on Friday that Particle had "doubled our compute capacity as of today," and that more people "keep finding particle-1 great to use due to token efficiency," he added that night. The work feels familiar, he wrote: "Particle has so many similarities to Mixpanel (scaling events vs tokens)." Asked whether users switch for the cost or the speed, he answered: "Both!"

Quick Hit

David Singleton published the first version of Poppy, an open standard for businesses serving people who use the Muse personal agent, built with industry partners. Muse is the first agent to support it.

V · Builders' Notebook

Personal Cybercabs, quantum-safe Ethereum and a 4-bit training run

Shorter items from the list on self-driving, cryptography, model training and wearables.

Cybercab for owners @aelluswamy · head of AI software, Tesla · @elonmusk

Tesla began inviting select customers to buy a Cybercab for personal use. Ashok Elluswamy wrote that it will "work unsupervised inside all the same geo locations as @robotaxi, just serving the owners’ needs," and confirmed that "it can park in your driveway." Musk said it "will be available nationwide, as we receive regulatory approval," but that the monthly fee is too high and "should match the cost of Tesla FSD and connectivity for other models, which is more like ~$115/month."

Vitalik Buterin @VitalikButerin · co-founder, Ethereum

Buterin explained why he has gone "from thinking leanPCS is total bull to being a total bull." Quantum-safe signatures grow from 65 bytes to 1 to 6 kilobytes and proofs to about 128 kilobytes, which is "unsustainable to put on chain." The proposal known as 8288 would aggregate them in the mempool, so only short claims reach the chain. Of three options, leanPCS, built on a polynomial commitment scheme, could arrive fastest, giving "a route to getting 'the cryptographic proof singularity' much faster than any of us expected." Separately, he proposed that any violence-detecting monitor be built from "open-source verifiable hardware" with an etched open-weight model and "no storage, no ability to load new algorithms."

Mira Murati @miramurati · CEO, Thinking Machines

Murati wrote that Thinking Machines trained Inkling 2 Small "with a native 4-bit forward pass on @nvidia GB300s, from the first pretraining step through the final RL rollout," one of the improvements that "took pretraining from weeks to 68 hours."

Palmer Luckey @PalmerLuckey · founder, Anduril

As "half of silicon valley" builds screenless AI wearables, Luckey said Humane's biggest mistake was "pretending it would completely replace your phone right off the bat," which forced "impossible price, performance, endurance, and thermal tradeoffs." It "Could have been a $99 phone accessory. (I have one)."

Takeaways

  1. SpaceX has now caught both stages of Starship, though not on the same flight. Making a ship reflyable without refurbishment is the next test.
  2. Most builders on the list want Huang back and blame thin sourcing, but Catanzaro, inside Nvidia, is backing the board, and Washington is pushing for an outsider.
  3. Tuesday's results and call are the first chance to hear Nvidia's interim leadership face investors.
  4. Open-source advocates answered Huang's exit with shipping code and a sovereignty pitch aimed outside the US.
  5. Agents are joining meetings and building software humans would not attempt, with verification built in from the start.
